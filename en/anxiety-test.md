## What you should know about this test.

### What is the GAD-7 anxiety test?

GAD-7 is a 7-question self-assessment questionnaire widely used by therapists and researchers for generalized anxiety screening. It's a validated, publicly available instrument.

### Is the anxiety test free and anonymous?

Yes. The test takes about 2 minutes, it's completely free, and your score is calculated directly in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server, and no name or email is required to see your result.

### Is this online anxiety test a diagnosis?

No. It's a self-assessment tool based on GAD-7, not a clinical evaluation. Only a licensed psychologist or therapist can diagnose. Your result shows whether your symptoms follow a pattern, not a medical conclusion.

### How common are anxiety disorders?

Common. The European ESEMeD study (Alonso et al., 2004) found that over 6% of adults have an anxiety disorder in a given year, and 13.6% over their lifetime. The WHO World Mental Health synthesis (Kessler et al., 2007) estimates lifetime prevalence between 9.9% and 16.7%, and the extended EU estimate (Wittchen et al., 2011) puts annual prevalence as high as 14%. Full citations are in the [sources section](#sources).

### How is the anxiety test score calculated?

The sum of your answers to the 7 GAD-7 questions gives a score from 0 to 21, split into 4 bands: minimal (0-4), mild (5-9), moderate (10-14), and severe (15-21).

### How often should I retake the anxiety test?

There's no fixed rule. If symptoms persist or change, you can retake the test periodically, for example every few weeks, to see if the pattern holds or shifts.

### What should I do if my score shows moderate or severe anxiety?

Consider talking to a psychologist or therapist. You can also read the free guide "5 things to know before your first therapy session", available above, right after your result.
